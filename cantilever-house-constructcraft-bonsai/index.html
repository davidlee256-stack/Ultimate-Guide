Cantilever House Prompts: ConstructCraft + Bonsai

Everything I used to have Claude Code build the same two-story house in two free Revit alternatives, ConstructCraft and Bonsai for Blender. Copy the brief, paste the prompts in order, and run the test yourself.

Step 1

The test brief

Save this as ~/revit-alt-test/brief.txt. Every prompt below tells Claude Code to read it. Writing it with coordinates is what let Claude place every exterior wall exactly.

CANTILEVER HOUSE - TEST BRIEF
Modern two-story house. Imperial units (feet).
Origin is the southwest corner; +X is east, +Y is north.

LEVELS
- Ground at 0 ft
- Upper at 11 ft

GROUND FLOOR (L-shaped plan, 10" exterior walls)
- Living wing: 60 ft x 24 ft, from (0,0) to (60,24).
  The west 20 ft is a double-height living room with walls rising to 21 ft.
  Other living wing walls are 11 ft tall.
- Guest and garage wing: 24 ft x 34 ft, from (0,24) to (24,58). Walls 11 ft tall.
- Ground floor slab under both wings.

UPPER FLOOR (the cantilever)
- Bedroom wing: 60 ft x 24 ft, from (20,0) to (80,24).
  Sits on top of the living wing and cantilevers 20 ft past its east end,
  with nothing underneath.
- Floor slab at 11 ft; 10" exterior walls, 10 ft tall.

COURTYARD
- Open courtyard from (24,24) to (60,58), enclosed by the two ground-floor
  wings and left open on the north and east sides.
- Pool from (48,26) to (56,56).

INTERIOR LAYOUT (5" interior walls, label every space)
Ground, living wing, west to east:
- Double-height living room (x 0-20)
- Dining (x 20-36)
- Kitchen (x 36-52)
- Pantry and powder room (x 52-60)
Ground, guest wing:
- Guest suite with bath (y 24-36)
- Two-car garage (y 36-58)
Upper floor:
- 6 ft corridor along the north side
- South of it: bedroom 2 (x 20-32), bedroom 3 (x 32-44), shared bath (x 44-52),
  primary bath (x 52-60), primary bedroom filling the cantilever (x 60-80)

STAIR
- Straight stair, 4 ft wide, against the north wall of the dining area
  from x 21 to x 36, rising from Ground to Upper.

Heads up: this brief has a flaw that Claude caught in both tools. The 4 ft stair lands inside the 6 ft upper corridor, leaving only about 2 ft to walk past. Leave it in to see if your AI spots it too.

Step 2

Before you start

I ran everything in the Linux terminal on a Chromebook. Any Linux, Mac or Windows machine with Claude Code works the same way.

  1. Install Claude Code and sign in.
  2. Make the folders: mkdir -p ~/revit-alt-test/{constructcraft,bonsai,exports}
  3. Download ConstructCraft for your system and extract it into ~/revit-alt-test/constructcraft.
  4. Download Blender 4.5 LTS, then install Bonsai from Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions.
  5. Start Claude Code in the test folder: cd ~/revit-alt-test && claude

The prompts use my file paths. If your ConstructCraft folder or Blender path is different, change those parts before you paste. Finish one tool completely before starting the other, and close the app before giving Claude each new prompt so you're not both editing the same file.

Step 3 · Tool 1

ConstructCraft prompts

ConstructCraft ships with a command-line tool called ccad, which Claude Code drives directly.

1

Learn the tool

ConstructCraft's command-line tool is at ~/revit-alt-test/constructcraft/constructcraft-v0.12.0-x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu/ccad. Run "ccad commands" and "ccad schema" for the wall, door, window, floor, roof and room commands. Tell me in plain English what you can build with it. Don't build anything yet.
2

Levels and exterior walls

Read ~/revit-alt-test/brief.txt. Using ccad, create a new project at ~/revit-alt-test/constructcraft/house.ccad in imperial units. Add two levels: Ground at 0 ft and Upper at 11 ft. On Ground, build the 10" exterior walls for the L-shaped plan exactly as the brief describes, including the 21 ft walls around the double-height living room. Remember ccad's command line takes lengths in meters, so convert carefully. Save the project and list every wall with its length and height in feet.
3

Upper wing, interior walls, stair, doors and windows

In house.ccad, following brief.txt: build the upper bedroom wing on the Upper level from (20,0) to (80,24) with 10" exterior walls 10 ft tall, cantilevering 20 ft past the east end of the living wing. Then add the 5" interior walls on both floors exactly as the brief lays them out, and the 4 ft straight stair from Ground to Upper. The interior walls on the living room's north side (y = 24, x 0-20) and east side (x = 20) must also rise to 21 ft for the double-height space. Add doors: a front entry, a two-car garage door, a door from the garage into the house, large sliding glass doors from the kitchen to the courtyard (x 36 to 52), and a door into every room. Add windows: at least one in every bedroom and the guest suite, plus a large window at the east end of the cantilever. Doors and windows must be hosted in walls. Save and list everything you placed.

In my run, Claude asked where the sliding doors should go because the stair sits on the dining room's courtyard wall. The prompt above already includes my answer: the kitchen wall.

4

Floor slabs, roofs and pool

In house.ccad, following brief.txt: add a ground floor slab under both ground-floor wings and a floor slab at 11 ft under the whole upper wing, with an opening for the stair. Add flat roofs over the upper wing, the double-height living room and the guest/garage wing. Leave the courtyard open and add the pool from (48,26) to (56,56) if ccad can model it. Save and tell me what you made.
5

Rooms and area check

In house.ccad, place a room in every enclosed space on both floors and name each one exactly as brief.txt does (living room, dining, kitchen, pantry and powder room, guest suite, two-car garage, corridor, bedroom 2, bedroom 3, shared bath, primary bath, primary bedroom). Report each room's area in square feet, the total per floor, and the total for the house. Save.
6

Drawing sheet and PDF

In house.ccad, set up the drawings with ccad: make sure there is a floor plan view for Ground and for Upper, then create a south elevation (showing the cantilever) and one east-west section cut along the stair so it shows the double-height living room and the stair together. Add a few overall dimensions to the plans. Create one sheet (the largest size ccad offers, landscape) with a title block titled "Cantilever House", and place both plans, the elevation and the section on it at sensible scales without overlapping. Export the sheet to vector PDF at ~/revit-alt-test/exports/constructcraft-sheet.pdf. Save the project and tell me each view's name and scale. If a step isn't possible from the command line, tell me which one.

ConstructCraft labels rooms automatically, and its sections are a single straight cut, which is why this asks for an east-west section.

7

Schedules

In house.ccad, create a door schedule (type, width, height, count) and a room schedule (name, area, total area). Export both to CSV in ~/revit-alt-test/exports/ as constructcraft-doors.csv and constructcraft-rooms.csv. Save.
8

IFC export

Export house.ccad to IFC4 at ~/revit-alt-test/exports/constructcraft-house.ifc. Then count how many walls, doors, windows, slabs, roofs and spaces are in the IFC file and report the numbers.

Step 4 · Tool 2

Bonsai prompts

Bonsai has no command-line tool, so Claude Code writes Python scripts and runs Blender in the background. Replace ~/apps/blender-4.5.16-linux-x64/blender with the path to your Blender 4.5.

1

Learn the tool

Blender 4.5 is at ~/apps/blender-4.5.16-linux-x64/blender and has the Bonsai add-on installed, which includes the ifcopenshell Python library. Write a small Python script and run it with "~/apps/blender-4.5.16-linux-x64/blender --background --python <script>" to confirm ifcopenshell imports and print its version. Then tell me in plain English how you'll build a BIM model as an IFC file. Don't build anything yet.
2

Levels and exterior walls

Read ~/revit-alt-test/brief.txt. Using Blender in background mode with ifcopenshell, create a new IFC4 project at ~/revit-alt-test/bonsai/house.ifc with imperial (feet) units. Add two building storeys: Ground at 0 ft and Upper at 11 ft. On Ground, build the 10" exterior walls for the L-shaped plan exactly as the brief describes, as real IfcWall elements joined at the corners, including the 21 ft walls around the double-height living room. Save the file and list every wall with its length and height in feet.

Check where it saved. In my run, Claude saved to the exports folder and needed one nudge to move the file to bonsai/house.ifc.

3

Upper wing, interior walls, stair, doors and windows

In ~/revit-alt-test/bonsai/house.ifc, following brief.txt: build the upper bedroom wing on the Upper level from (20,0) to (80,24) with 10" exterior walls 10 ft tall, cantilevering 20 ft past the east end of the living wing. Then add the 5" interior walls on both floors exactly as the brief lays them out, and the 4 ft straight stair from Ground to Upper. The interior walls on the living room's north side (y = 24, x 0-20) and east side (x = 20) must also rise to 21 ft for the double-height space. Add doors: a front entry, a two-car garage door, a door from the garage into the house, large sliding glass doors from the kitchen to the courtyard (x 36 to 52), and a door into every room. Add windows: at least one in every bedroom and the guest suite, plus a large window at the east end of the cantilever. Doors and windows must be IfcDoor and IfcWindow elements filling real openings in their walls. Save and list everything you placed.
4

Floor slabs, roofs and pool

In ~/revit-alt-test/bonsai/house.ifc, following brief.txt: add a ground floor slab under both ground-floor wings and a floor slab at 11 ft under the whole upper wing, with an opening for the stair. Add flat roofs over the upper wing, the double-height living room and the guest/garage wing. Leave the courtyard open and add the pool from (48,26) to (56,56) if it can be modeled. Slabs and roofs must be real IfcSlab and IfcRoof elements. Save and tell me what you made.
5

Rooms and area check

In ~/revit-alt-test/bonsai/house.ifc, place a room in every enclosed space on both floors and name each one exactly as brief.txt does (living room, dining, kitchen, pantry and powder room, guest suite, two-car garage, corridor, bedroom 2, bedroom 3, shared bath, primary bath, primary bedroom). Rooms should be IfcSpace elements with area quantities. Report each room's area in square feet, the total per floor, and the total for the house. Save.
6

Drawing sheet and PDF

Using Blender in background mode with the Bonsai add-on, load ~/revit-alt-test/bonsai/house.ifc and set up the drawings with Bonsai's own drawing tools: a floor plan for Ground and for Upper, a south elevation (showing the cantilever), and one east-west section cut along the stair so it shows the double-height living room and the stair together. Make sure each plan shows room names and a few overall dimensions. Create one sheet (the largest size available, landscape) with a title block titled "Cantilever House", and place both plans, the elevation and the section on it at sensible scales without overlapping. Bonsai makes drawings and sheets as SVG; convert the finished sheet to PDF at ~/revit-alt-test/exports/bonsai-sheet.pdf (install a converter such as rsvg-convert or Inkscape if needed, and tell me what you used). Save the IFC project and tell me each view's name and scale. If a step isn't possible from the command line, tell me which one.

Bonsai's default drawing style leaves out door swing arcs. If you need them, ask Claude to turn them on.

7

Schedules

From ~/revit-alt-test/bonsai/house.ifc, create a door schedule (type, width, height, count) and a room schedule (name, area, total area). Save them as CSV in ~/revit-alt-test/exports/ as bonsai-doors.csv and bonsai-rooms.csv.
8

IFC export

Copy ~/revit-alt-test/bonsai/house.ifc to ~/revit-alt-test/exports/bonsai-house.ifc. Then count how many walls, doors, windows, slabs, roofs and spaces are in it and report the numbers.
You

Bonus: swap the files

  1. Open exports/constructcraft-house.ifc in Blender with Bonsai (File > Open IFC Project).
  2. Import exports/bonsai-house.ifc into ConstructCraft.
  3. Click a wall, a door and the stair in each one to see what came across as real, editable elements. Then check the units.

Save yourself some time

Tips from the video

Tip 1: Write your brief with coordinates. Give every wing a start point and an end point, in feet or whatever units you use. That's why Claude put every exterior wall exactly where the brief said.

Tip 2: App opens but won't take clicks? On a Chromebook or any Linux setup, put WAYLAND_DISPLAY= with nothing after it in front of the launch command. It fixed both apps for me:

WAYLAND_DISPLAY= ~/revit-alt-test/constructcraft/constructcraft-v0.12.0-x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu/constructcraft
WAYLAND_DISPLAY= ~/apps/blender-4.5.16-linux-x64/blender

Tip 3: Bonsai needs Blender 4.5, not Blender 5. If Bonsai won't install, that's almost always why.

Tip 4: Check the units after an IFC swap. Both models were built in feet and both opened in metric in the other program. If you send IFC files to a consultant, ask them to check the units when they open it.
