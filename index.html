01

Nobody is paying you to evaluate AI software.

Something launches every week promising to redraw your plans, write your reports, or pull a scheme out of a sketch. A few are genuinely useful. Sorting those from the rest is unbilled research, and it lands on whoever was curious enough to try.

  1. i

    The demos aren't project work.

    Launch videos run on clean, invented briefs. Your model carries four consultants' revisions and a client who rewrote the brief in month seven. Tools behave differently there, and the difference is the whole story.

  2. ii

    Nobody publishes the failures.

    Vendors show you the output that worked. What matters is the third attempt, and whether correcting it took longer than doing the job by hand.

  3. iii

    The cost of finding out lands on you.

    A trial account, an afternoon of setup, a week of fighting the output, then the quiet decision to go back to the old way. That is the real price of evaluating a tool — and you pay it per tool.

This book is that work already done: what held up, what didn't, and which failures to expect before they cost you an afternoon.

The 3 saved prompts

The full pipeline from the video. Build the setup once — Custom Instructions, one Project per job, these three prompts — and stop re-typing your instructions on every project. Each one assumes you're working inside a ChatGPT Project with your own files uploaded: the brief, your code and zoning documents, your drawings. That's what turns the model from something that remembers into something that reads.

Prompt 01

The Brief

Turns raw call notes into a structured brief you can design against — with every line marked stated, implied or missing, so you can hand it straight back to the client for confirmation.

SOURCE
Use only my raw notes and the files in this Project. Do not add
requirements, preferences, standards or budget figures that
aren't in them. Do not fill gaps with what's typical for this
project type.

TASK
Turn my raw notes into a structured architectural brief I can
design against and send back to the client for confirmation.

STRUCTURE
- Project summary, three sentences
- Client goals: what they want, and where stated, why
- The people: who lives or works here, how many, how they use
  the space
- Site: address, orientation, access, slope, existing structures,
  neighbours, anything they mentioned about the setting
- Program: every space named, with any size, quality or
  adjacency note attached to it
- Constraints: planning, zoning, heritage, access, services,
  timeline
- Budget signals: figures given, and any indirect signal
  (finishes named, comparisons made, phrases like "nothing fancy")
- Priorities: what they'd protect if the budget tightened, and
  what they'd drop
- Timeline and any fixed dates

TRACEABILITY
Against every line, mark one of:
STATED - quote the words from my notes
IMPLIED - my notes point to it, say which line and how
MISSING - not in the notes at all
Never write a line without one of these marks. If a section is
entirely MISSING, say so rather than producing a plausible one.

QUESTIONS
Then a numbered list of every question I still need answered,
ordered by what blocks design work soonest. For each, say in one
line what it changes if the answer goes either way.

CONTRADICTIONS
List anywhere my notes conflict with each other, or where a
stated goal doesn't fit a stated constraint or budget signal.
Quote both sides. Don't resolve it.

OUTPUT
The brief, then the questions, then the contradictions. No
preamble, no summary of what you did.

Prompt 02

The Code & Zoning Checklist

Only works once your jurisdiction’s documents are uploaded to the Project. Every line comes back with a section number and a word-for-word quote, or it comes back marked “not in sources.”

JURISDICTION
City / county: [    ]        State: [    ]
Building code adopted: [    ] edition, with [state] amendments,
effective [date]
Residential code, if separate: [    ]
Fire code: [    ]   Energy code: [    ]   Accessibility: [    ]
Zoning ordinance: [    ], last amended [date]
Zoning district: [    ]   Overlays: [    ]
Use group / occupancy: [    ]   Construction type: [    ]
Storeys: [    ]   Sprinklered: [yes / no]
Anything blank above: don't assume it and don't use a national
default. Add it to the missing documents list and stop.

SOURCES
Use only the documents uploaded to this Project. Do not use
general knowledge of building codes, and do not rely on anything
you remember about this jurisdiction.
Before the checklist, list every document you'd need but don't
have, and name it specifically enough that I can go find it.

PRECEDENCE
Where documents overlap, the more local one governs: local
amendment over state amendment, state amendment over model code.
Where a local amendment changes a model code figure, show both,
say which governs, and cite both sections.
Where the zoning ordinance and the building code both touch an
item, such as height, treat them as separate requirements and
list both.

SCOPE
Egress and travel distance, stair and guard geometry, corridor
and door widths, room sizes, ceiling heights, light and
ventilation, fire separation and ratings, structural and energy
where the documents cover it, setbacks, height, lot coverage and
FAR, parking and loading, and accessibility.

FOR EACH ITEM
- The requirement, in plain language
- The governing number or threshold
- Document name, section number, and page
- A word for word quote of the sentence that number comes from
- Status: VERIFIED, quoted from an uploaded document
           NOT IN SOURCES, needs verification
           CONFLICT, two documents disagree, show both and cite both
- Whether it's a hard limit or subject to a listed exception, and
  quote the exception
- How this project stands against it, if the brief or drawings in
  this Project give me the figure. Otherwise mark it as unchecked.

RULES
Never cite a section you can't quote. If the section number and
the quote don't match, output NOT IN SOURCES instead.
Never paraphrase a number into the quote field.
Don't mark anything verified on the strength of a table alone
without naming the table and its conditions.

OUTPUT
Missing documents first. Then the checklist, grouped by building
code and zoning. Then a short list of every item where a local
amendment changed the model code figure, because those are the
ones I'm most likely to get wrong from memory.

Prompt 03

The 2D to 3D Model Handoff

Fill the geometry block once per project. Anything left blank gets added to the missing list instead of being guessed — and the dimension report tells you exactly which numbers to check.

SOURCE
Use only the floor plan, elevations and program in this Project.
Do not invent dimensions, rooms, levels or openings that aren't
on the drawings.

BEFORE YOU MODEL
List everything missing that would change the geometry: floor to
floor height, wall and partition thicknesses, slab thickness,
door and window head and sill heights, roof form and pitch, level
changes, north orientation, and drawing scale. Ask for those. Do
not pick values to fill the gaps.

GEOMETRY
Units: [mm / m / ft-in]. Work in this unit throughout.
Floor to ceiling height: [    ]
Floor to floor height: [    ]
Exterior wall thickness: [    ]
Interior partition thickness: [    ]
Slab / floor thickness: [    ]
Door head height: [    ]
Window head height: [    ]   Window sill height: [    ]
Roof: [flat / pitched at    / not modelled]
Number of levels: [    ]
Anything left blank: do not assume a value and do not use a
regional default. Add it to the missing list and stop.
Take every other dimension from the drawings, not from typical
construction. Where a drawing gives a figured dimension, that
figure wins over anything scaled.

OUTPUT
An editable SketchUp model. Every element a named component or
group, tagged by material, organised so I can lock the shell
independently of the interior.
Named scenes for each elevation, plus plan and one isometric.

VERIFY
Return a dimension report with the overall footprint, each room's
clear dimensions and area, and every thickness used. Mark every
value you inferred rather than read off the drawing.
Where the plan and the elevations disagree, list the conflict.
Don't resolve it silently.

The rule that holds all three together. Every stage produces a draft, not a deliverable. Verify every code citation against the published document, overlay every model against your real site dimensions, and read every client-facing word before your name goes on it. The AI doesn't stamp drawings. You do.
